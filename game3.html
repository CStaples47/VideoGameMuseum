About the Exhibit

Mortal Kombat drops fighters from many realms into a deadly tournament, with the fate of Earth at stake. Players pick a warrior, such as the ninja rivals Scorpion and Sub-Zero, the thunder god Raiden or the movie star Johnny Cage, and battle through a ladder of opponents toward a multi-armed boss.

The first game stood out in arcades for its look. Instead of hand-drawn characters, it used digitized images of real actors, which gave the fighters a gritty, realistic style that was very different from its cartoonish competition.

Exhibit Highlights

  • Fatalities: After winning a match, players could enter a secret button sequence to perform a dramatic and exaggerated finishing move. Learning the codes became a playground rite of passage.
  • Special moves: Every character had signature attacks performed with joystick motions and button presses, which rewarded practice and memorization.
  • Rich mythology: Realms, sorcerers, tournaments and rivalries built a surprisingly deep story that grew with every sequel.
  • Mini-games and secrets: Bonus challenges like the strength-testing break-the-blocks game, plus hidden characters, kept players hunting for rumors and codes.
  • Lasting legacy: The series has kept going for decades with sequels, a reboot, movies and television adaptations.

Curator's Note: The game's graphic style sparked public debate in the early 1990s. That controversy helped lead to the creation of the video game industry's age-rating system in the United States, which is still used to label games today.

Why It Is One of My Favorites

Mortal Kombat is pure arcade attitude. It is dramatic, a little silly and surprisingly deep once you learn each fighter's moves. Winning a close match, or finally nailing a tricky special move, never gets old.
