About the Exhibit

GoldenEye 007 turned the plot of a Bond film into a mission-based shooter. You play as the British secret agent, sneaking through dams, bunkers, jungles and trains. Unlike many shooters of its time, each mission came with specific objectives, such as disabling alarms, photographing evidence or protecting an ally, and the list grew longer on harder difficulty settings.

Many people thought first-person shooters belonged on a computer with a keyboard and mouse. GoldenEye showed that a console controller could handle aiming, strafing and precision shots just fine, and it paved the way for the genre on home systems.

Exhibit Highlights

  • Mission objectives: Three difficulty levels (Agent, Secret Agent and 00 Agent) changed the goals, not just the enemy toughness, which gave each level real replay value.
  • Stealth and gadgets: Silenced pistols, watches with special abilities and security cameras made sneaking a real option instead of just running and gunning.
  • Location-based damage: Enemies reacted differently depending on where you hit them, which made aiming feel meaningful.
  • Split-screen multiplayer: Up to four players could battle on one TV, with a mix of weapons, maps and rule variations.
  • Cheats to unlock: Beating levels under a target time unlocked fun cheats, adding a speedrunning flavor to the single-player game.
Curator's Note: Many people remember GoldenEye as the game their whole friend group gathered around. Arguments over who got to be the best character and whether proximity mines were fair became the stuff of legend.

Why It Is One of My Favorites

The mix of tension in the single-player missions and chaos in the multiplayer matches is hard to beat. Even now, a four-player match with friends feels like a time machine to an era when you had to be in the same room to play together.
